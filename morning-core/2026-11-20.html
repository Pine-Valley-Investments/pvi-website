November 20, 2026

Everyone Wants
to Buy the Dip

The S&P 500 fell for a sixth straight session and all major indexes are below their 200-day averages; ANT is down 13% premarket near its $83 IPO price after trading as high as $111 yesterday. Atlanta Fed business inflation expectations reached 3.5%, the highest since May 2025, while oil and refining margins fall. Per Nomura, vol-control funds would add about $62.9B over a calm month but sell $23.2B on a single 5% down day.

Nicholas Lampone
Chief Strategy Officer & Investment Advisor · Pine Valley Investments

U.S. Futures & World Markets

Anthropic's highly anticipated IPO couldn't stop the tech slide yesterday. The S&P 500 fell for the sixth straight trading session (see chart below) with all major indexes now trading below their 200-day technical levels. Investors continue to reprice the AI trade as the competition for capital, and the debate over how much all this spending will ultimately generate in returns, drags on.

ANT is down 13% premarket and is trading near its IPO price of $83. For context, the stock ticked as high as $111 yesterday, and closed near $97. FOMO around IPOs never ceases to amaze me. Apparently, yesterday's buyers are already looking for the exit.

The risk-off trade continues as technology stocks search for a bottom. It's another reminder that market bottoms aren't formed in a day. They're usually a process, and that process can be uncomfortable.

A good friend shared a quote with me yesterday that I think sums up the current market well: "Everyone wants to buy the dip, until it's time to buy the dip."

Charts & Data

Firms surveyed by the Atlanta Fed expect inflation of 3.5% over the next 12 months, the highest since May 2025. Atlanta Fed via Daily Chartbook (business inflation expectations).

Wells Fargo's misery index soars to a generational high. Wells Fargo via @mikezaccardi and Daily Chartbook (misery index).

The oil price has NEVER dropped this fast unless the economy was in a complete crash. Is that really what we are amidst? It certainly doesn't feel like it. This tells me that this is supply driven rather than demand driven. @andreassteno, Steno Research via Daily Chartbook (oil prices).

More important (for central banks and bond investors at least) than the drop in crude prices is the huge drop in global oil refining margins. From a peak of about $60-$75 a barrel only weeks ago, benchmark refining margins have fallen to about $25-$40, in some cases approaching pre-war levels. Javier Blas, Bloomberg via Daily Chartbook (refining margins).

At about $3.75 per gallon, gasoline hasn't been this cheap in America since March 15, a couple of weeks after the start of the US-Iran war ... prices are now approaching the crucial $3.50-a-gallon level; researchers have found that the cost of the commodity largely disappears from news coverage - and thus from public conversation - below that threshold. Javier Blas, Bloomberg via Daily Chartbook (gasoline price).

At this point, the fall in gold isn't about rising real rates in the US, but about panic and liquidation. You can see that from the chart below, where it's the fall in the S&P 500 (orange bars) and my fear factor (pink bars) driving the drop in gold. @robin_j_brooks via Daily Chartbook (gold prices).

Active manager exposure dropped by the most in over a year and now sits at its lowest since April 2025. NAAIM Exposure Index via Daily Chartbook.

Hedge fund net exposure to US semis and semi equipment has now fallen below software exposure for the first time since the start of 2026. Goldman Sachs via Zero Hedge and Daily Chartbook (hedge funds vs. semis vs. software).

Per Nomura's grid, vol control funds would add roughly $62.9 billion over the next month if daily moves average close to zero, but the same cohort dumps $23.2 billion on a single 5% down day. A single bad day resets the clock. Charlie McElligott, Nomura via Zero Hedge and Daily Chartbook (vol control funds).

The last time the S&P 500 fell for 6 consecutive sessions was more than 2 years ago. Daily Chartbook Studies (S&P 500 losing streak).

Third consecutive day under the 200-day MA. This would be the 22nd instance since 2005. Historically, the Index was higher 62% of the time 10 days later and 71% of the time 21 days later. The worst decline was following the Nov. '07 instance, which was the peak before the financial crisis. @andrewthrasher, Thrasher Analytics via Daily Chartbook (S&P 500 technicals).
