Nvidia, Mortgages,
and Smoking on Airplanes

NVDA +16% premarket on strong earnings — Salesforce, Okta, CrowdStrike also jump. Software catching a bid. In 2010, a new home cost 3.3 years of average income. Now it's 6.0 years. Corporate profits hit an all-time high in Q2 — up 22.8% YoY. Mag 7 EPS grew 119% YoY. Other 493 grew 32% — best since Q4 2021.

Nicholas Lampone
Chief Strategy Officer & Investment Advisor · Pine Valley Investments

U.S. Futures & World Markets

It's all about Nvidia this morning after NVDA posted strong earnings and guidance. Technology stocks are jumping, with semiconductor and AI-related names leading the way higher.

It's nice to see software stocks participating in this rally. Salesforce, Okta, and CrowdStrike are all up after reporting strong earnings/guidance. The bigger question is whether this is just a relief rally or something that can be sustained longer-term.

On housing: in 2010, it took the average American 3.3 years of gross income to buy a new home. Now, the ratio is 6.0 years. Crazy stuff.

Charts & Data

Nvidia jumped to $222 premarket — the stock was down 7 straight sessions going in, so the print came at a moment of maximum pessimism. TradingView: a textbook bear trap.

Energy positioning increasingly crowded — any genuine geopolitical progress could produce a meaningful unwind across both oil and linked equities. Goldman via Daily Chartbook: the most vulnerable crowded trade right now.

Positive earnings breadth near previous cyclical highs — Yardeni doubts the "circular financing" AI bubble narrative. Yardeni Research: the earnings story is real.

Mag 7 grew EPS 119% YoY in Q2. Excluding Alphabet and Amazon, growth drops to 43%. The Other 493 rose 32% — best since Q4 2021. FactSet via Daily Chartbook: the broadening of earnings growth is the most important market story of Q2.

The growth gap between Mag 7 and Other 493 is expected to flip in favor of the Other 493 by Q4. FactSet: the case for continued sector rotation is strengthening.
