November 6, 2026

Stocks Are
"Off the Wall"

Happy Soul Train Friday. The Nasdaq had its worst day since March 2020 and is in correction (QQQ -11.6%); ~$3T of S&P 500 market cap wiped out in a single day. Semis fell at least 17.5% in five days — a move seen only in the Asian crisis, Russian default, dot-com bust, 2008 and Covid. VIX hit its first panic level (27.50). Nomura: S&P 500 return dispersion at the 100th percentile of 30 years.

Nicholas Lampone
Chief Strategy Officer & Investment Advisor · Pine Valley Investments

U.S. Futures & World Markets

Happy Soul Train Friday! https://mcore.hopp.to/youtube396

Stocks are trying to bounce this morning after yesterday's carnage. The Nasdaq (technology stocks) Index had its worst day since the Covid crash in March of 2020 and is officially in correction territory. QQQ is down -11.6%, and an official "correction" is considered a decline of more than 10%. For some perspective on how swift and painful this sell-off has been, we saw $3 trillion in S&P 500 market cap wiped out in a single day. Not exactly a great day in the office.

The Democratic sweep has raised fears of tighter AI regulation and the possibility of a slower pace of data center development. Semiconductor stocks were already weak going into the election, and Tuesday's results accelerated the puke in tech stocks. On the bright side, semis are bid up premarket. Lam Research (LRCX) is +11% after authorizing a $25 billion share buyback program and declaring a quarterly dividend of 33 cents per share.

It's almost an afterthought this morning, but earnings season remains in full swing. Cloudflare is +20% after topping Q3 estimates. Airbnb is +14% on a beat and raise. Moderna is +4% premarket after raising its fiscal 2026 revenue growth outlook. DraftKings plunged 20% on an earnings miss and guidance cut.

It was encouraging to see the VIX hit its first panic level (27.50) yesterday, indicating an initial wave of seller exhaustion. After a week of election-driven volatility and a pretty painful tech unwind, a little stabilization would be welcome. Let's see if the bulls can get this tape to finish the week on a positive note.

Charts & Data

Risk appetite for Tech has crashed close to Liberation Day levels, according to our Tech risk appetite measures — and it won't take much for that to recover. Daniel von Ahlen, TS Lombard via Daily Chartbook.

Retail investors on track for the largest day of selling across single stocks since the COVID drop. Vanda via @kevrgordon.

Today's selloff, combined with the explosion in volatility, is bringing CTA selling back into play. A clean break below 26,800 (which happened today) would likely see CTA exposure collapse toward Liberation Day lows, adding another layer of selling pressure to an already stressed market. Vanda Research via Zero Hedge.

The S&P 500 and Russell 2000 fell by the most in over a year and a half. The Nasdaq-100 and Semiconductors had the worst days since Covid. Daily Chartbook.

Asian Financial Crisis. Russian Debt Default. Dot-Com Bust. 2008 Financial Crisis. Covid Crash. Those are the only other periods since 1994 when semiconductors fell at least 17.5% in five days. Bespoke via Daily Chartbook.

Semiconductor stocks are now oversold for the first time since the tariff tantrum in April 2025. Daily Chartbook Studies.

Post-midterms, the question is whether the bull market can continue without semis participating. This year, owning the S&P 500 on days the semi industry is positive has produced a 70% gain. On semi down days, the S&P 500 has lost 34%. No industry has dictated index performance this much. 3Fourteen Research via @warrenpies.

VVIX over 120 today — when VVIX is above 125, the SPY has seen strong positive forward 10- and 20-day returns. @spotgamma via Daily Chartbook.

Over the past month, the S&P 500 moved 1.1% while the average stock moved 13.2%. This spread ranks in the 100th percentile over the past 30 years. The only higher return spreads were during the Dot-Com Bust and GFC. Nomura via @marlin_capital.

Low correlations make for dramatic sector-level moves that can create macro questions, but in the end we take them as a bullish sign about investor confidence in the US economy and even more so toward US large-cap stocks as an asset class. Nick Colas, DataTrek Research.

The S&P 500 is now 3.5 standard deviations below its 50-DMA, the most oversold it has been since the COVID Crash, yet 62% of stocks in the index remain above their 50-DMAs. Bespoke via Daily Chartbook.

The equal-weight S&P 500 closed at a three-year high on November 4, in under 63 sessions after a 5%+ pullback. After similar repairs, the next three months were higher 83% to 93% of the time. SentimenTrader via Daily Chartbook.

The rotation out of AI names into other sectors is impacting reactions around earnings. Technology and Communications are among the few sectors posting negative excess returns even on earnings beats; by contrast, Discretionary and Materials have gained even after posting misses. 22V Research via Daily Chartbook.

Markets are also suspicious of the AI earnings boom: look how far PEG ratios have fallen for the big seven AI firms relative to other sectors and markets. Michael Cembalest, JPMorgan via Daily Chartbook.

The biggest market issue may be AI over the medium term: nearly every major local ballot measure opposing data centers won or is winning, with the only exception in North Dakota. This is not an issue for the 37 GW expected to be delivered in 2027, 75% of which is under construction. It's more an issue for the 96 GW expected in 2028. Michael Cembalest, JPMorgan via Daily Chartbook.
