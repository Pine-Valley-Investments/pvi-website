BTFD Is Back.
Scotch, Scotch, Scotch

Nasdaq +2% premarket as the buy-the-dip crowd returns; CoreWeave tops estimates and raises capex guidance. A hot CPI has a 25bp December hike fully priced, with a 50bp hike at 14%. ETF investors absorbed ~$12B/day over five days, with the top 17 ETFs all equity. S&P realized vol hit the 100th percentile and vol-control funds can't add risk back. Tech and Communication Services are 47.6% of S&P market cap and 47.1% of forward earnings.

Nicholas Lampone
Chief Strategy Officer & Investment Advisor · Pine Valley Investments

U.S. Futures & World Markets

Tech stocks are bouncing again this morning with the Nasdaq up nearly 2% premarket. The Buy-the-Dip crowd is back (see charts below), and they're bottom-fishing in beaten-up AI names. CoreWeave is helping sentiment after topping third-quarter revenue expectations and raising its fiscal 2026 revenue and capital-spending guidance. Apparently, the dip is looking a little more attractive again.

Inflation is back in the spotlight after yesterday's CPI report came in hotter than expected. The bond market has a 25 bps rate hike priced in for December, which is a near certainty. The bigger question is what happens in 2027, and that's why we've seen accelerated volatility in Treasuries. Investors are trying to figure out where rates go from here, and the answer isn't exactly clear.

I'll end with some interesting commentary from Nick Colas of DataTrek Research on current P/E multiples, sector valuations, and the AI trade: The S&P 500 trades at a modest (8 pct) premium to its 25-year average forward 12-month PE multiple. Eight sectors have better valuations now than their long run history, but not Tech. Its forward multiple is -14 pct lower than its 2½ decade average, thanks in part to greater cyclicality (semis exposure), but also concerns about how the AI trade will develop. Being "cheap" is not enough to justify liking Tech, but its history of leveraging disruptive innovation to extract incremental profits supports a bullish view regardless of where multiples go from here.

The AI trade has certainly become more complicated, but the longer-term opportunity hasn't necessarily gone away. As always, there's a difference between short-term market sentiment and the ability of great businesses to create value over time.

Charts & Data

Food and energy came in hot. But if you exclude that, goods came in hot. But if you exclude that, shelter came in hot. But if you exclude that, the rest of core non-housing services came in hot. If you exclude that, then, well, there's nothing left. @mtkonczal via Daily Chartbook (CPI).

The share of core CPI components rising at a 3%+ annualized pace leapt to 59% in October, from 51% in September. @samueltombs via Daily Chartbook.

Nominal average hourly earnings were up 3.1 percent in October from a year ago, which means that no matter what year-over-year number you use for inflation, prices have risen faster than wages over the past year. @bencasselman via Daily Chartbook (an important chart on wage growth and inflation).

The AI buildout by itself has raised GDP by only about 0.3 percent. The stock market boom that came with it matters more — together they have raised GDP by about 0.6 percent and lowered the unemployment rate by about 0.3 percentage point. If the stock market gains go away, they could take much of that growth with them. Jason Furman via Daily Chartbook.

Far from the job killer many feared, AI has so far been an engine of job growth. Since ChatGPT launched four years ago, the economy has added 4.1 million jobs. By our estimates, AI is responsible for 786,000 of them, or nearly one-fifth, net of the jobs lost to AI substitution. AI's contribution has grown over the past year to 284,000 of the 943,000 jobs created, or nearly one-third. Mark Zandi via Daily Chartbook.

Markets have fully priced a 25bp rate hike in December on the back of Waller's comments and CPI data. A 50bp hike is also on the table at 14%. @interest_rates via Daily Chartbook.

Even before today's move in Treasury yields, bond investors were already demanding extra compensation (spread) in return for lending money to data center projects. Alexander Altmann, Barclays via Tracy Alloway, Bloomberg.

One-month S&P realized vol is at 22.1, a 100th percentile reading, versus 14.7 for three-month. With last Thursday's 4.4% drop locked into that one-month window for weeks to come, vol-control funds can't add risk back. Their implied equity allocation has slid further to $116.1 billion, an 11.2 percentile reading over three years, down from $124.6 billion last week. Charlie McElligott, Nomura via Zero Hedge.

Current Tech positioning prices in more than just a sharp deceleration. It now reflects outright negative earnings growth and deep estimate cuts. Deutsche Bank via @isabelnet_sa.

The Schwab Trading Activity Index rose to a fresh 4-year high in October as investors bought the dip in Tech stocks, and semis in particular. Schwab via Daily Chartbook.

ETF investors didn't just buy the dip, they devoured it — about $12B/day in flows over the past 5 days, with the top 17 ETFs all being equity. Vanguardians and degens are very different people playing very different games, but they definitely agree on one thing: you buy the dip. @ericbalchunas via Daily Chartbook.

Last week we saw the elusive 80% NYSE downside volume day for the first time in nearly a year. An 80% downside day is often a necessary part of a bottoming process, but given it's been almost a year without one, we should not be surprised to see another, if not several, before all is said and done. Jonathan Krinsky, BTIG via Daily Chartbook.

When Tech has lagged the S&P by 2 standard deviations in the past, the results have been mixed, but the downside has been limited. What happened next depended on the catalysts. Nick Colas, DataTrek Research via Daily Chartbook.

IT and Communication Services together account for 47.6% of the S&P 500's market capitalization and a nearly identical 47.1% of its forward earnings. In 2000, when valuations were at tech-bubble extremes, their market-cap share far exceeded their earnings share. Ed Yardeni, Yardeni Research via Daily Chartbook.
