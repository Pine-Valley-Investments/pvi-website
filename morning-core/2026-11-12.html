November 12, 2026

Leather Jackets, AI &
the Cost of Capital

Stocks slip on reports that Nvidia's board is considering replacing Jensen Huang; oil consolidates on hopes for a diplomatic solution to the Iran war. For tech to move meaningfully higher, the market needs to know who is financing the AI buildout and at what cost. Robinhood single-stock buying on Tuesday was the third highest on record, and semis fell 20%+ then rallied 15%+ in four sessions.

Nicholas Lampone
Chief Strategy Officer & Investment Advisor · Pine Valley Investments

U.S. Futures & World Markets

Stocks are under pressure after yesterday's bounce, following reports that Nvidia's board is considering replacing Jensen Huang. The wolves are coming for Jensen and his black leather jacket. The WSJ reported he had a second extramarital affair and a relationship with an Nvidia executive. Not exactly the kind of headlines investors want to see surrounding one of the most important companies in the AI trade. These headlines are putting renewed pressure on tech stocks and weighing on sentiment.

Leaving the gossip pages for a bit, oil is consolidating at lower levels as the market focuses on a possible diplomatic solution to the Iran war. We're also seeing headlines about another push for an end to war in Ukraine. Toss in hotter inflation data, retail dip buying (see chart below), and midterm results, and it's understandable we're living in a volatile tape. Plenty of moving parts, and not all of them are related to earnings.

While the near-term headlines remain salacious, it pays to focus on the important drivers of long-term returns. The AI buildout carried the tape earlier this year, and we've since seen defensive sectors outperform in the second half of 2026. For tech stocks to move meaningfully higher from current levels, we need to find out who is financing the AI buildout, and how much the hyperscalers are paying for that financing.

Ultimately, the market needs to see a return on all that spending. That's a much bigger story than whatever happens to Jensen's leather jacket.

Charts & Data

Stocks and bonds are no longer necessarily moving in opposite directions during risk on/risk off ... Consider the last 100 trading sessions. Stocks and bonds either both rallied or both declined in 62 of those sessions. @jeffweniger via Daily Chartbook (Corgi Invest).

Is the AI sell-off the start of a bear market? In hiking cycles, bears came when rates hit stocks & balance sheets together. When only one did, it stopped at a correction. Today only rates are weighing - which continue to suggest this is just a correction. @prometheusmacro via Daily Chartbook.

UBS estimates CTA global equity exposure at -5%, the 5th percentile over the past year and 23rd percentile over five years ... The positioning reset has been extreme ... A sustained rebound could force CTAs back into equities, adding another powerful tailwind to the squeeze. UBS via Zero Hedge and Daily Chartbook.

Robinhood traders apparently felt fine fighting the Fed by buying the dip: net daily purchases of single stocks [on Tuesday] were the third highest on record, trailing only June 12 (#1, SpaceX's IPO) and September 23 (#2). Luke Kawa, Sherwood via Daily Chartbook.

The S&P 500 could spend the next 69 trading sessions going sideways between $730 and $780, and its 200-day moving average would keep rising every day. Even if every close was at the bottom of that range. Grant Hawkridge, The Daily Number via Daily Chartbook.

The Semiconductor index fell by over 20% during the 6 sessions from 10/29 to 11/5, then rallied by more than 15% over the next 4 sessions (through today). Reversals of this magnitude have only happened 3 other times: near the 2000 top, near the 2008 bottom, and near the Covid lows. Daily Chartbook Studies.

AI spending rose to a new peak in October, rising 1.65% from September. Spend also increased among the top 1% of spenders. The top 1% of firms now spend a median $10.2K per employee per month on AI, up 9%. Firms in the top 10% spent $801, roughly flat. The median firm spends $16.32, up $0.93. Ara Kharazian, Ramp via Daily Chartbook.

GPUs are melting. On-demand B200 index rose 1.9% to a new high of $6.97 on Nov 10 and H100 1.7% to a new high of $3.06; B300 ($7.68), H200 ($3.48) and A100 ($1.59) were unchanged, but hovered near ATHs and poised to follow. Recent price surges point to a clear demand inflection. @silicon_data via Daily Chartbook.

A slower US build does not reduce demand for compute, it reallocates it, and the cheapest marginal supplier with spare power is China. Robert Taylor, TS Lombard via Daily Chartbook. A great reminder that compute demand won't slow down just because the US doesn't want to build data centers.

Global earnings growth is still in the 30s: Q3 growth moderated to 33%, still one of the highest rates on record. Tech, Energy and Materials did much of the heavy lifting, but growth excluding them was still a robust 12%. The strength is broadening: 7 of 11 global sectors delivered double-digit earnings growth. Jim Reid, Deutsche Bank via Daily Chartbook.

US small caps are set to accelerate: earnings growth is forecast to climb from 36% in 2026 to 45% in 2027. For the S&P 500, earnings growth is expected to slow from 34% to 16%. Goldman Sachs via Daily Chartbook.
