November 16, 2026

Chicken Wings, AI,
a $1.4 Trillion IPO

Tech drags futures lower on the Nvidia fallout and concerns around AI spending; the Fed's financial stability report flags risks on elevated prices, partly driven by AI. Per Jones Trading, Anthropic is expected to price Nov. 18 at $70-$78 per share, a $1.3-$1.45 trillion valuation. US equity funds saw $51.6B of inflows, the most in two months.

Nicholas Lampone
Chief Strategy Officer & Investment Advisor · Pine Valley Investments

U.S. Futures & World Markets

Technology stocks are dragging the tape lower this morning as the Nvidia fallout and concerns around AI spending weigh on sentiment. A financial stability report from the Federal Reserve isn't helping matters, either. The Fed said it sees risks on elevated prices, partly driven by AI. Reports that Jensen Huang is already lobbying for a possible return to Nvidia are adding another layer of uncertainty to an already messy situation.

As for the Anthropic IPO, here is an update from Jones Trading: Anthropic is expected to price its initial public offering (IPO) on Wednesday, Nov. 18. It will begin trading under the ticker symbol "ANT" on Thursday, Nov. 19. In an amended S-1 filing released Tuesday, Anthropic said it expects to sell 825 million shares priced between $70 and $78. That would give the company a market valuation of between $1.3 trillion and $1.45 trillion. Anthropic was initially projected to hit the market with a valuation as high as $2 trillion, but recent market turmoil in the AI sector has knocked that down significantly.

I was reading an article in the WSJ on Wingstop, and how the restaurant chain is using Amazon Web Services and AI to forecast customer demand. Here is an interesting quote: So Wingstop developed a machine-learning model that forecasts customer demands and runs on AWS technology. "It's ingesting weather forecasts, school calendars, betting lines around games to gauge excitement factors," said Skipworth. "All this comes in, and this thing operates at a level of precision that is scary."

Even the chicken wing business has entered the AI era. This is a good example of how AI is moving beyond the headline-grabbing technology companies and finding its way into everyday businesses. The investment question, as always, is whether all this spending can translate into productivity gains, better margins, and earnings growth. That's what will matter when the dust settles.

Charts & Data

The Nasdaq 100 is also rebounding from oversold levels. Apparently every time the RSI went below 30 during November since inception, the Nasdaq rallied an average 5%. Trouble is it's already up precisely 5% off the 5-Nov close. Rebounds are one thing, next steps are another thing. Callum Thomas (Nasdaq RSI rebound).

Semis and software walked a similar path for a long time, but the AI age saw semis sharply pull away. Since peaking in June semis are off some 30%, and now sitting at a key support level. I would highlight that something which has already fallen 30% could still fall another 30% ... which is certainly not a forecast, but a caution against blind-buying something just because it fell a big percent. Callum Thomas (semis & software).

Speaking of semis, on a price to 2-year forward earnings basis, the SOX is now almost as cheap as it was back in 2011, cheaper than 2020 and 2022. ... The market does not believe either or a combination of: the earnings estimates get realized, or said earnings are sustainable. (But then again, you could say similar things back in 2022 before one of the biggest bull markets in recent years took off ... so maybe we don't overthink it too much?) Callum Thomas (deep value in semiconductor stocks).

Rising stock prices "because AI" has had a bigger impact on the economy than the actual building of datacenters has. A big part of this is how household allocations to equities have risen to record high levels, and even a bigger weighting than real estate - the wealth effect for stocks is a huge deal. It's also why the economy will probably struggle to withstand the effects of a major downturn in stocks. Callum Thomas (AI boom economic impact).

My favorite PPI measure is Stage 4 intermediate demand: inputs for goods & services on the cusp of being finished to be sold to consumers & businesses. Since it's immediately upstream of final demand, it gives a sense of last-mile price pressures. It grew 8% year-on-year in Oct. @ernietedeschi via Daily Chartbook (PPI).

The Producer Price Index brought a little bit of relief following the CPI data released earlier this week. Translating the details of PPI and CPI, we estimate that core PCE will climb 0.28% MoM in October, or 3.4% SAAR, well above the Fed's target. @renmacllc via Daily Chartbook (core PCE estimate).

Hyperliquid whales just flipped back to net long on $BTC. Over the last two weeks they were short; their first short exposure since early 2026. They were short from the top and long through the whole August pump. Will they be right again this time? @glassnode via Daily Chartbook (Bitcoin whales).

US equity funds saw inflows of $51.6bn, the most in 2 months. BofA via @mikezaccardi and Daily Chartbook.

Mon-Wed, NVDA took 61% of US retail net buying. @vandaresearch via Daily Chartbook.

In the five prior episodes since 2011 where SMH was down 25%+ and XLK 15%+ from their 52-week highs, SPY was already in a double-digit drawdown every time. Today it's down just 3%. Daily Chartbook (the S&P 500 is hanging tough, even as semiconductors take it on the chin).

BETI has triggered an equity buy signal after remaining mostly in risk-off mode since late May ... Based on a 10-year historical analysis, the S&P 500 is expected to gain about 8% over the following two months. The indicator has a 95% historical hit rate over that period. Barclays via Natalia Kniazhevich, Bloomberg and Daily Chartbook (Barclays Market Timing Indicator).

Excess liquidity has deteriorated sharply, and historically this has been a pretty good leading indicator for equities. The relationship is far from perfect, but the message is worth paying attention to. Bloomberg via Zero Hedge and Daily Chartbook (SPX vs. excess liquidity).

The valuation premium of the largest US companies has narrowed ... tightest in almost 10 years. Goldman Sachs via @mikezaccardi and Daily Chartbook (the Top 5 vs. the bottom 495).

With 91% of the index reported, the S&P 500's blended YoY growth rate for Q3 is 31.9%, which would mark the 3rd straight quarter of +25% growth and the 8th consecutive quarter of double-digit growth. John Butters, FactSet via Daily Chartbook.

The market is rewarding beats by more than usual and punishing misses by less than usual. John Butters, FactSet via Daily Chartbook (earnings reactions).

Goldman estimates that hyperscaler capex contributed 12 percentage points to S&P EPS growth this year, up from 5pp in 2025 and 2pp in 2024. That fades to 9pp in 2027 as depreciation bites, and then flips to a 5pp drag in 2028. Goldman Sachs via Zero Hedge and Daily Chartbook (AI capex vs. EPS growth).
