Capitulation, Relief Rally,
Tower of Terror

Futures bounce after Friday's late-day capitulation sent the VIX to 35.90; the S&P 500 took just 33 trading days to fall 10% from its Oct. 6 peak, among the fastest corrections on record. Per BTIG, after prior seven-day losing streaks of -5% or more, five-day forward returns were positive nine of ten times. BofA: eight straight weeks of US equity fund inflows.

Nicholas Lampone
Chief Strategy Officer & Investment Advisor · Pine Valley Investments

U.S. Futures & World Markets

Stocks are FINALLY bouncing this morning after Friday's selloff, which featured a healthy dose of late day capitulation. Sellers were indiscriminately dumping anything and everything, and the VIX spiked to 35.90. After that kind of washout, it's not surprising we're seeing a pretty significant bounce today. When everyone rushes for the exits at the same time, eventually you run out of sellers.

If you thought this down move in the market was fast, it's not your imagination. A Bloomberg chart below shows one of the fastest corrections (10% drop) on record. There's an old Wall Street saying that stocks take the stairs up, and the elevator down. It feels like we've been on Disney World's "Tower of Terror" ride lately - an elevator ride from hell, with no warning before the bottom drops out.

Nick Colas of Datatrek Research made an interesting observation about what's being priced into the current market: "At current levels, the market is pricing in a combination of sustained higher interest rates, aggressive AI regulation, disappointing returns on AI infrastructure investment, and prolonged uncertainty around some of the most important companies in the technology sector. There is a lot of room for positive surprise heading into 2027." It doesn't take much for sentiment to improve when expectations get this depressed.

While it's encouraging to see today's positive action in this relief rally, I wouldn't confuse it with a "coast is clear" signal yet. As I mentioned last week, market bottoms are a process, and volatility rarely spikes this high and then immediately disappears. As for the bounce, here is a stat from Krinsky at BTIG: SPX closed Friday down for its seventh straight day. Since 1980, this is the tenth 7-day losing streak that lost -5% or more. Five-day forward returns have been positive nine of ten times.
