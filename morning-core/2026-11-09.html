November 9, 2026

Can the
Bounce Stick?

Tech bounces and crude falls, but XLK fell 8.2% last week and the Nasdaq declined for a 4th straight week. Nvidia -5% premarket on reports about CEO Jensen Huang; the board is investigating. October CPI, SoftBank earnings, TSMC sales and the Anthropic IPO roadshow all arrive tomorrow. S&P 500 forward P/E 17.7 — the lowest since Nov 2023; Tech's 16.9 is the lowest since March 2020.

Nicholas Lampone
Chief Strategy Officer & Investment Advisor · Pine Valley Investments

U.S. Futures & World Markets

Stocks are higher premarket as tech stocks bounce and crude oil moves lower. The market is looking to build on Friday's recovery, and technology stocks will be the key to whether this rally has any legs.

Crazy headlines on Nvidia and Jensen Huang (see below) have NVDA down 5% premarket. Here is the official statement from Nvidia's board: "While the Board has no reason to believe that either national security or NVIDIA secrets were compromised, the Board's inquiry will examine all available information."

It's understandable to see the premarket recovery after last week's debacle. The XLK (tech ETF) was down -8.2% on the week, and the Nasdaq declined for the 4th straight week. Some stabilization here would certainly help sentiment.

Tomorrow we'll get earnings from SoftBank and sales data from Taiwan Semi, along with the October CPI report. The Anthropic IPO roadshow is also set to kick off tomorrow. Plenty of moving parts, and not much chance of a quiet start to the week.

S&P Futures vs. Fair Value: +68.00  |  10-Year Yield: 4.85%

Charts & Data

Consumer sentiment improved modestly in the preliminary November reading. Inflation expectations for the year ahead fell to 4.3% from 4.7%. @neilksethi via Daily Chartbook.

A pullback in AI stocks could hit household wealth and weigh on spending. Positive wealth effects have added 0.4 percentage point to US GDP over the past year. Goldman via @isabelnet_sa.

It's always tough to ignore what's happening in credit when looking at equities, and some of the most AI-exposed names have come under heavy pressure in recent sessions. We've had plenty of warnings from the credit space, but the two seem more in sync now. We'll have to see if credit goes any further. Jim Reid, Deutsche Bank via Daily Chartbook.

Thursday was the biggest put day in CBOE history. @zerohedge via Daily Chartbook.

Barclays' Alexander Altmann estimated that Thursday was the largest-ever selling requirement from levered ETF rebalancing in history (so far). Barclays via Tracy Alloway, Bloomberg. (Nick: another example of how awful these products are for investors, unless used strictly for day trading.)

Nomura estimates roughly $61B of implied vol-control selling following Thursday's shock. These strategies typically react with a lag, meaning selling pressure may extend into subsequent sessions. Nomura via Zero Hedge.

According to Barclays, volatility-control funds could reduce equity exposure by about 30 percentage points, one of the largest one-day adjustments since the Covid-era turmoil, and could sell roughly $135B in US equity futures over the next 24 to 48 hours. Barclays via Natalia Kniazhevich, Bloomberg.

US-listed ETF shorts jumped 7% on Thursday, the largest one-day increase since March and the third largest in the past five years, led by shorting in large-cap equity ETFs. Lee Coppersmith, Goldman via Zero Hedge.

It's one thing for non-AI stocks to outperform AI stocks for a week or two, but this rotation has been going on for a while. Over rolling six-month periods, the differential is now the worst we've seen during this four-year bull market. Sonu Varghese, Carson Group via Daily Chartbook.

Since QQQ began trading in early 1999, there have only been 15 prior days when it fell 10%+ below its 50-DMA without having done so in the prior 50 trading days. Bespoke via Daily Chartbook.

An indicator that measures the spread between the VIX Index and whichever of its futures contracts has the highest price: negative readings are rare, and show a bottoming condition for prices. @mcclellanosc via Daily Chartbook.

The S&P 500's forward P/E multiple fell to 17.7, below the 5- and 10-year averages and the lowest since November 2023. John Butters, FactSet via Daily Chartbook.

At the sector level, six sectors had forward 12-month P/E ratios on November 5, 2026 that were below their 5-year averages, and six were below their 10-year averages. John Butters, FactSet via Daily Chartbook.

Waterfall decline continues: the Tech sector's forward P/E has fallen to 16.9, the lowest since March 2020. @kevrgordon via Daily Chartbook.

GPU rental rates are still unmistakably rising across the board. Whatever the stock market is nervous or fearful about is not what's happening right now but the odds of what will happen in the next couple of years. @silicon_data via Daily Chartbook.
